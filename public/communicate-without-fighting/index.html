How to Talk to Your Partner Without It Turning Into a Fight

Simple communication tips for couples: soft starts, timing, I-statements and how to cool down mid-argument.

Most of our fights were never about the dishes. They were about how I brought up the dishes. These are the habits that cut our arguments in half.

Start soft

The first sentence predicts how the talk will go. "You never help" starts a war. "I'm overwhelmed tonight, can you take the dishes?" starts a fix.

Pick your moment

Not when he walks in the door. Not when either of you is hungry. Ask, "Is now a good time to talk about something small?"

Use one complaint at a time

Don't stack the last six months onto tonight.

Say what you need, not just what's wrong

"I need ten minutes of hearing about your day" is something he can do. "You don't pay attention to me" is something he can only defend against.

Take a real break

If your heart is pounding, take 20 minutes. Say when you'll come back. Then do.

Repair attempts count

A joke, a touch, "okay, I hear you" — accept them when he offers.

Respect is the secret ingredient

Many men hear criticism as "you're failing me." Pairing a request with respect ("you're so good at this, could you…") changes the reaction completely. It's the main idea behind a guide I wrote about in my His Secret Obsession review.

Practice line

"I feel ___ when ___. What I'd love is ___. Can we try that?"
