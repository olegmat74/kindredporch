The 5 Love Languages Explained (With Real Examples for Couples)

A simple guide to the five love languages: words, time, gifts, acts of service and touch, with everyday examples.

The love languages idea is simple: people feel loved in different ways, and we tend to give love the way we want to receive it. That mismatch explains a lot of "but I do so much for you" arguments.

1. Words of affirmation

Spoken or written appreciation. Examples: a note in the lunch bag, "I'm proud of how you handled that," telling a friend something good about him while he can hear.

Want to understand what he isn't saying out loud? Watch the free His Secret Obsession video before you spend anything.

2. Quality time

Undivided attention. A walk without phones, cooking together, asking a question and listening to the whole answer.

3. Receiving gifts

Not about price. It's about being thought of. A favorite candy bar from the gas station counts.

Love languages are a start, not the whole map

4. Acts of service

Doing things that lighten the load: warming up the car, handling the call he hates making, folding the laundry he started.

For more on why small words of respect land so hard with men, see what James Bauer teaches.

5. Physical touch

Hand on the back while passing in the kitchen, sitting close on the couch, long hugs.

How to find yours (and his)

  • Notice what you complain about most. It often points to what you're missing.
  • Notice how he shows love. People usually give in their own language.
  • Ask: "What's something I did this week that made you feel cared for?"

Where it gets more interesting

Many men don't fit neatly in one box. Under the language, there is often a need to feel respected, trusted and useful to the person they love. When that need is met, the love languages almost take care of themselves. I looked at one program built around that need in my His Secret Obsession review, and it filled in what this framework leaves out.

A one-week experiment

  1. Each day, do one small thing in his language.
  2. Don't announce it.
  3. At the end of the week, notice how he responds, and ask him to try it for you.
