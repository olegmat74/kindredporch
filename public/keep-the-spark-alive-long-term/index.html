How to Keep the Spark Alive in a Long-Term Relationship

Practical ways to keep romance going after years together: novelty, appreciation, flirting and small daily rituals.

Twelve years in, I can tell you the spark doesn't vanish. It gets buried under groceries, school pickups and who forgot to pay the water bill. These habits are how we dig it back out.

Do new things together

Novelty releases the same chemistry you had at the start. It doesn't have to be skydiving. A new hiking trail, a cooking class, a different coffee shop on Saturday. New places make you see each other fresh.

Flirt like nobody is keeping score

Send the text you would have sent in year one. Wink across the kitchen. Compliment something specific: the way he fixed the gate, how he talks to your mom.

The idea that finally made this click for me came from James Bauer's free presentation on the hero instinct.

Keep a six-second kiss

A quick peck is a habit. A slow six-second kiss goodbye is a moment. Try it for a week.

Protect a weekly date

Same night, every week, even if it's at home. (Here are 21 ideas for evenings in.)

If you want the why behind this, His Secret Obsession explains what makes a man feel needed, and it changed how I phrase things.

Admire him out loud

Men rarely hear they are admired. When you tell him what you respect about him, you feed something that keeps him close. I explain this idea, and the program built around it, in my review of His Secret Obsession.

Have a life of your own

Your own friends, hobbies and goals make you more interesting to come home to. Mystery is not games, it's having a full life.

Repair fast

Every couple fights. Long-term couples just repair faster: "I'm sorry I snapped. Can we start over?"

Small rituals that add up

  • Coffee together before phones.
  • A two-minute check-in at dinner: high and low of the day.
  • A shared playlist you both add to.
  • Holding hands on walks.
