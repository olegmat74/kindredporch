Healthy Boundaries in Relationships: Examples and How to Set Them

What healthy boundaries look like in a relationship, examples for time, money, phones and family, and how to say them kindly.

Boundaries used to sound cold to me. Now I think of them as instructions for loving me well, and asking him for his.

What a boundary is (and isn't)

A boundary is about what you will do. "If the yelling starts, I'll leave the room and we can talk later." It isn't a rule for him: "You can't see your friends."

Examples by area

  • Time: "I need Sunday mornings for myself."
  • Phones: "No phones at dinner, for both of us."
  • Money: "Let's talk before any purchase over $200."
  • Family: "We decide holiday plans together before telling our parents."
  • Emotional: "I'm not okay being teased about my weight, even as a joke."
  • Physical: "I need a few minutes of quiet after work before hugs."

How to say it kindly

  1. Pick a calm moment (see how to talk without fighting).
  2. Share the why.
  3. Keep it short.
  4. Ask about his.
  5. Follow through gently.

Red flags

If your boundaries are mocked, punished or constantly pushed, that's a pattern worth taking to a counselor.
