52 Deep Questions to Ask Your Boyfriend (That Don't Feel Like an Interview)

Thoughtful questions to ask your boyfriend or husband: childhood, dreams, money, love and silly ones for road trips.

The best conversation my husband and I had last year happened in a parked car outside a Safeway, because I asked him one odd question: what did you want to be at nine years old? Forty minutes later the ice cream was soft and I knew things about him I had not heard in twelve years.

The idea that finally made this click for me came from James Bauer's free presentation on the hero instinct.

Good questions do that. They are not a quiz. Pick two or three, ask them when you are side by side (driving, walking, cooking), and let silence do some of the work.

Childhood and roots

  1. What did you want to be at nine?
  2. Which adult made you feel most understood as a kid?
  3. What is a smell that sends you straight back to childhood?
  4. What rule in your house growing up seems strange now?
  5. What were you proud of that nobody noticed?
  6. Who taught you how to be a man, for better or worse?
  7. What holiday tradition would you keep forever?

Dreams and the future

  1. If money were handled, how would you spend a Tuesday?
  2. What is a skill you secretly want to learn?
  3. Where do you picture us at seventy?
  4. What would you regret not trying?
  5. Which of your goals do you talk about least?
  6. What does a "good year" look like to you?
  7. What would make you feel successful, in your own words?

Love and us

  1. When did you first feel at home with me?
  2. What small thing I do makes you feel respected?
  3. What do you wish I asked you about more often?
  4. How do you know when I'm upset, before I say it?
  5. What is one way I could support you this month?
  6. Which memory of us do you replay most?
  7. What does feeling needed mean to you?

That last one matters more than it sounds. A lot of men will never volunteer it, but feeling useful and trusted is close to the center of how they attach. I wrote more about that idea in my honest review of His Secret Obsession, which is built around it.

Money and work

  1. What did your parents teach you about money without meaning to?
  2. Saver or spender, honestly?
  3. What job taught you the most?
  4. What would you do if you could quit tomorrow?
  5. What purchase still makes you happy?
  6. What is a money fear you have never said out loud?

Light and silly (road-trip safe)

  1. Which fictional character are you most like?
  2. What is the worst haircut you ever had?
  3. What would your wrestling entrance song be?
  4. Pancakes or waffles, and defend it.
  5. Which animal would you be for a day?
  6. What is a hill you would die on?
  7. What food could you eat every day?
  8. What would your perfect sandwich be?
  9. Best concert you ever went to?
  10. Which decade would you live in?

For slow evenings

  1. What are you most grateful for this week?
  2. When do you feel most like yourself?
  3. What is something you have changed your mind about?
  4. Who do you miss?
  5. What do you want more of in your life?
  6. What advice would you give your twenty-year-old self?
  7. What scares you a little about getting older?
  8. What makes a friendship last?
  9. What is your favorite way to be comforted?
  10. When was the last time you cried?
  11. What song feels like us?
  12. What would you like to be remembered for?
  13. What is a small win you never celebrated?
  14. What do you love about your own life right now?
  15. What question do you wish I had asked?

How to ask without it feeling heavy

One question per evening is plenty. Answer it yourself too. If he gives a short answer, try "say more about that" once, then let it go. Curiosity is the point, not completion.
